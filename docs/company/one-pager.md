# Sage OS — one page

**An AI that knows your work, on your own computer.**

**The problem.** Everyone is told AI agents will change how they work, but for most people "agents" are a buzzword:
something for developers with terminals, API keys and config files. The AI they do use starts from zero every time:
their documents, notes and past conversations are scattered, so the answers are generic. And handing real work to an
AI feels risky: what did it change, and can it be undone?

**The product.** A desktop app for Windows and Mac that anyone can install:

1. **Pick an AI:** a free model that runs on the computer itself, or ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude.
2. **Add your documents:** they go on the Bookshelf; answers come from them, with sources.
3. **Switch on helpers:** a morning brief, a weekly recap, meeting prep. They work on a schedule and bring results
   to an Inbox, where nothing is saved without your OK and everything can be undone.

**Why it's different.** Private by design (the data never leaves your devices, and devices sync with each other
directly, with no cloud and no account); works with any AI, including free local ones; and "agents" are concrete
helpers with an approval inbox, not a chat box.

**Why now.** Small open models now run well on ordinary laptops; people already pay for and juggle several AIs;
and trust in where personal and company data goes has become a buying criterion.

**Where it stands (October 2026).**
- Working app on Windows and Mac with signed automatic updates; 41 releases since the first commit on 30 Sep 2026.
- Built: the Bookshelf (search by meaning, AI-written notes you approve, exact undo), one chat for every AI, connect
  guides with costs per AI, device pairing, a problem-report flow, a first-time-user pass, and a first screen built
  to reach a useful answer in about five minutes.
- 380 automated tests; every release is checked on clean Windows and macOS machines before it ships.
- First outside demo (an investment professional, 14 Oct 2026) asked for recurring jobs, meeting briefs, a
  note-taker and team libraries: all now on the roadmap.

**Next.** Ready-made helpers (U3); a download page with no terminal steps (U1; signed installers later); then sync
between a person's devices.
